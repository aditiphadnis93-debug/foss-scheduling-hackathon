# Replit prompt: Court Time Planner prototype

Build a web app called **Court Time Planner**. It helps a High Court judge and their court master decide which cases to list each day, in what order, and at what time. The people using it are **judges and court masters, not engineers**. They should never see formulas, probabilities or model terms. They should see their day, their cases, and clear, visual reasons for every suggestion. The app **recommends**; the judge or court master **always makes the final call**.

## Tech setup

- **Frontend:** React + Vite + TypeScript + Tailwind. Use a small component library such as shadcn/ui, and Recharts or plain SVG for charts. Build the Gantt/timeline views as custom SVG or divs, not a heavy library.
- **Backend:** Python FastAPI. I will upload `engine.py` (a stdlib-only scheduler and simulator), JSON rule presets, and the court's CSV files. Wrap the engine in API endpoints. Don't rewrite its logic in the frontend.
- **Storage:** start with SQLite or in-memory state. No login for now; use a mock user "Justice Sehgal", role Judge, with a toggle to switch to Court Master.
- Seed the app with the uploaded `roster_3000.csv` and `court_calendar.csv` so every screen has realistic data on first load.

## The workflow

A left sidebar menu, in this order. Each step should nudge the user toward the next one.

### 1. Home (dashboard)

- **Greeting:** "Good morning, Justice Sehgal. Tomorrow: 34 matters recommended · 7 hours of sitting."
- **Four plain-language cards, each with a trend arrow:**
  - Cases that moved forward this week
  - People sent home unheard
  - Cases older than 4 years (count, and whether it's going down)
  - Days until your roster period ends
- **"Needs your attention" list** of flagged cases, each with an icon and a one-line reason. Flags:
  - 4+ years old
  - Adjourned 3+ times in a row
  - Stuck at one stage far longer than usual
  - Waiting on a warrant or summons
- A primary button: **"Prepare tomorrow's cause list →"**

### 2. Calendar & leave

- A month view of court working days, weekly offs and holidays, taken from `court_calendar.csv`.
- The judge can mark personal leave days. Leave days are removed from scheduling, and any cases on them get re-planned with a visible notice.
- Sitting hours are editable. The default is 10:00–13:30 and 14:00–17:30, which gives 7 hours with a lunch break.

### 3. Roster

- Upload or replace the roster CSV (the organisers' schema). Validate it, and show errors in plain words.
- After upload, show a summary:
  - cases by stage (a horizontal bar per stage)
  - cases by age (under 1 year, 1–3, 3–4, 4+)
  - how many are ready to be heard vs waiting on something
- A searchable, filterable case table (stage, age, advocate, flag). Clicking a case opens a **case card** with:
  - a timeline of its past hearings
  - its current stage
  - what it's waiting for
  - its next recommended date and why

### 4. Priorities (the judge's rules)

- **Preset cards** to pick from:
  - **Recommended (balanced)**
  - **Block scheduling:** same weekday every week until heard
  - **Group by advocate & purpose**
  - **New matters first**
- **Simple controls in plain words, never numbers like "fill factor":**
  - **How full should the day be?** Light / Balanced / Packed. Under the control, show the consequence live, e.g. "Packed: about 3 more cases heard a day, but about 8 more people sent home unheard."
  - **Time kept for old cases (4+ years):** a slider with a **lock icon at its minimum (25%)** and the tooltip "Older cases can't be pushed back below this. It's a court-wide rule."
  - **Keep each advocate's matters together:** on/off
  - **Order of the day:** "Complex hearings first, short matters after" (default) or the reverse
- Changes save as a named rule set and immediately update the preview in step 5.

### 5. Create cause list (the core screen: spend the most care here)

- **Choose the period:** next day / next week / next month. For a week or month, show a calendar strip where each day has a small bar showing how full it is. Clicking a day opens that day.
- **A recommended number of matters,** with a one-line reason: "34 matters fills your 7 hours, allowing for the ones that usually don't go ahead."

**Day timeline (Gantt)**

- The horizontal axis is the time of day (10:00–17:30), with lunch shaded.
- Each case is a bar. Its **width** is its expected duration. Its **colour and icon** show the hearing type (Arguments, Evidence, Judgement, Appearance, Bail…). Use a colour-blind-safe palette, and always pair colour with an icon and a label.
- **Default order: complex, substantive hearings first** (arguments, evidence, judgments), **simpler and newer matters in the second half.** If the day runs late, the matters that slip to tomorrow are the short ones, where a delay hurts least. Show this as a short caption on the timeline.
- Each bar shows its **30-minute appointment window**, which is what the advocate and litigant are told.
- A **"likely to go ahead"** indicator per case: High / Medium / Low, shown as filled dots, never as a percentage.
- A shaded **"overflow zone"** at the end of the day: "If the day runs long, these move to tomorrow, first in line."
- **Group bars by advocate** where possible, with a subtle bracket saying "ADV-011 · 3 matters back to back".

**Why this case?** Clicking any bar opens a side panel with the plain-language reasons it's listed today, each with an icon, for example:

- ⏳ "Waiting 4 years 2 months: older cases get priority"
- ⚖️ "Next step is judgment: close to disposal"
- ✅ "Warrant served on 12 Sept: ready to proceed"
- 👥 "Same advocate as the two matters before it: one trip"
- ↩️ "Not reached yesterday: carried forward to the top"

**"Not today" lane.** Below the timeline, a list of cases held back, each with its reason and the date it's expected to be ready:

- "Warrant not yet returned: ready around 14 Oct"
- "Next step can't happen before 9 Oct (14-day gap after plea)"
- "Advocate has 4 matters in Court 3 tomorrow"
- "Day is full: recommended for Thursday"

### 6. See the impact (what-if)

- Whenever the user changes rules or moves a case, show **"Your choice vs recommended"** side by side, as big plain cards with arrows and colour plus icons:
  - cases heard
  - cases that move forward
  - people sent home unheard
  - old cases touched
  - hours used
- **Backlog outlook:** a simple line chart of "cases older than 4 years" over the next 8–12 weeks, one line for the recommendation and one for the user's choice.
- **Who's affected:** a list of cases pushed to a later date by this choice, with old date → new date and the advocate, and a count of advocates who now need an extra trip.

### 7. Finalise & publish

- Drag and drop bars on the timeline to reorder cases, or drag a case onto another day in the week strip. After every move, show an instant impact badge, e.g. "+1 person likely sent home" or "No impact".
- Overrides are always allowed. Optionally ask for a short note ("Reason for change"), and keep an audit trail of what the judge changed versus the recommendation.
- **Publish:**
  - generate a printable, court-style cause list (PDF or print view) grouped by block, with appointment windows
  - a stub "Notify advocates" button that shows a preview of the SMS/WhatsApp message with their window and what to bring

## API endpoints to wrap the engine

- `POST /roster/upload` → validation result and summary
- `GET /cases`, `GET /cases/{id}` (with flags and reasons)
- `GET/POST /calendar/leave`
- `GET/POST /rules` (presets and custom rule sets)
- `POST /schedule/preview` with `{period, start_date, rules}` → per day: the ordered cases with start time, window, block, likelihood (high/med/low), reason codes, and the held-back cases with reason codes and ready dates
- `POST /schedule/impact` with `{proposed_schedule}` → metrics vs recommended, backlog projection, affected cases
- `POST /schedule/publish`

**Reason codes.** Return reasons as **codes** (e.g. `OLD_CASE`, `NEAR_DISPOSAL`, `READY_PROCESS_SERVED`, `CARRIED_OVER`, `SAME_ADVOCATE`, `WAITING_WARRANT`, `GAP_NOT_ELAPSED`, `DAY_FULL`, `ADVOCATE_CLASH`, `LEAVE_DAY`) with parameters. The frontend maps each code to an icon, a colour and a plain-language sentence, so the wording can be tuned without touching the backend.

## Design principles

- **Plain language everywhere.**
  - "Likely to go ahead", not "P(substantive)"
  - "People sent home unheard", not "reach rate"
  - "How full should the day be?", not "fill factor"
- **Every recommendation has a visible reason.** No black boxes.
- **The judge decides.** Recommendations are defaults, not locks. The only locked rule is the minimum time for old cases, and it's shown with a lock icon and a one-line explanation.
- **Calm, formal, trustworthy look:**
  - neutral background, one accent colour
  - large readable type (16px base)
  - generous spacing
  - high contrast, keyboard accessible
  - light and dark mode
- **Never use colour alone:** always colour + icon + label.
- **Desktop first** (the court master's PC), usable on a tablet in court.
- **Progressive disclosure:** summary first, details on click.

## Build order for the first iteration

1. Seed data + backend endpoints for preview and impact, wrapping `engine.py`
2. Screen 5 (cause list timeline + "why this case" + "not today" lane)
3. Screen 6 (impact comparison)
4. Screen 4 (priorities with live consequence text)
5. Home, Roster, Calendar, and Finalise/Publish (the notification can be a stub)

## Done when

- A court master can open the app, pick "next week", and see each day as a timeline with reasons.
- They can move one case to another day and immediately see who's affected and what it costs.
- They can print the final cause list.
- No screen shows a formula, a probability or a model term.

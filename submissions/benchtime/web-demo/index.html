How exactly

We calibrated the world by proportional fitting on world seeds 1-20 of the 3,000-case roster, which scales up PUCAR's 100-case sample with seed 42. The reached-weighted mean absolute error in P(substantive) is 0.2%. The failure shares sit within two standard errors of PUCAR's estimates.

Common random numbers key every case's draws by roster id and world seed, so two policies see the same attendance and the same process returns. The search used seeds 1-6. We decided on seeds 7-20, confirmed on 21-30 and took the headline only from 31-60. Each run covers 1 October to 15 December 2026.

The pick, g237

It ranks cases by one priority index and fills 111% of the day, with a standby list. It gives each next date on the earliest day with room after PUCAR's gap. The desk handles matters whose summons has not come back. Every case over 4 years old comes before the bench at least every 45 working days.

How exactly

The zoo meta-policy has 41 genes, including one gene of five objective weights. They cover the selection rule, first dates, the next-date rule, coverage, the desk, check-in, calibration, the fill target, the standby share, five objective weights, the ageing floor and its exponent, rotation days and the relist rules. Presets reproduce today's way, the three judges and the textbook rules (first come first served, bin packing, oldest first).

The search mixed random genomes and preset mutants, then ran NSGA-II (non-dominated sorting with crowding distance) on tuning seeds 1-6. The plot shows the 946 distinct genomes that ran fresh under the final rule on the shared court, on tuning seeds 1-6.

19

guardrails must each be "no worse than today". Among the schedules that pass, the most useful hearings win. We fixed this rule at 14:15, before anyone ran seeds 31-60.

How exactly

The original rule maximised useful hearings a day behind 7 guardrails. Some guardrails compared against the best baseline instead of today's way, and a coverage limit relaxed itself to whatever the search reached.

The final rule is a paired non-inferiority test against today's way on the same seeds. A guardrail passes when the 95% t-bound of the candidate minus today clears its margin. Absolute limits compare means. No limit relaxes itself. We decided on seeds 7-20, confirmed on 21-30 and took the headline from 31-60 only.

What we found

On 30 test runs it had never seen, our list gives 6% more useful hearings and breaks a third fewer dates.

We lose on one count. 140 more cases go the whole quarter without a hearing.

Our pick stays ahead of today in all 12 stress tests.

Four choices do the work: the priority index, correcting PUCAR's judgment table, the standby list and spreading first dates like today.

Held-out seeds 31-60, which are 30 simulated quarters from 1 October to 15 December 2026, on PUCAR's synthetic sample court. Each bar is our pick minus today's way, with its 95% interval.

How exactly

Each difference is paired on the same 30 world seeds, with a 95% t interval on 29 degrees of freedom. Shared seeds pair the courts. Across seeds, the pick and today correlate at 0.61 on useful hearings a day and at 0.94 on merits disposals.

On seeds 31-60, 18 of 19 guardrails hold. The rule said in advance that we report a failure and keep the pick. The stress tests switch off behaviour responses, set the duration spread to 0.25 and 1.0, slow process returns by half, double false alarms, bias the planner by 30% either way and swap in five other rosters.
